Commercial Cleaning Quotation Checklist for Karachi Businesses

By SafaiKaro · September 2026 · 8 min read

A usable commercial cleaning quote states the exact scope, what is excluded, who supplies materials, the access window, the schedule, and the payment terms, all tied to one price. This checklist works against any vendor's quote in Karachi, not only SafaiKaro's. Copy it, share it with whoever is collecting quotes for your office, shop or building, and score each vendor against the same list before you compare a single number.

How to use this: Send this checklist to every vendor you are getting a quote from and ask them to answer it in writing. If a vendor cannot or will not answer a section clearly, treat that as information, not just an inconvenience.

Karachi buying friction is specific: a security desk that wants crew CNICs before granting entry, a building on a water tanker schedule rather than mains supply, and load-shedding hours that stop a floor scrubber mid-job. A quote that has not accounted for any of this on paper usually has not accounted for it on the day either.

1. Scope: What Is Actually Being Cleaned?

The single biggest source of quote confusion is an unclear scope. Two quotes with wildly different prices are often describing different amounts of work, not different vendors' margins.

  • Rooms and areas. Which specific rooms, floors or zones are included by name, not "the office" as a whole?
  • Surfaces. Floors, desks, accessible glass, washrooms, pantries, high-touch surfaces (door handles, switches, railings)? List each one that matters to you.
  • Depth of clean. Is this a light tidy-up or a deep clean (grout, skirting, under furniture, inside cabinets)?
  • Shared or common areas. If the building has shared corridors, lifts or lobbies, are they included or is this unit-only?
  • Specialist items. Anything needing height access, specialist equipment or a separate skill (kitchen exhaust, HVAC ducts, exterior glass) usually needs its own scope, not a line item buried in a general quote.

2. Access and Timing

  • Access window. What hours can the crew work, and does the space need to be vacated during the clean?
  • Building access. Are there security, parking, loading or lift restrictions the vendor needs to plan around? In a plaza or gated commercial building, has the vendor confirmed the crew's CNIC details are submitted to the security desk in advance, so the visit is not held up at the gate?
  • Utilities. Is water and power reliably available on-site for the crew to use, or does the vendor bring their own? Ask specifically what happens during load-shedding hours or on a day the building's water tanker has not arrived, since either can stall a wash-down or floor-scrubbing job midway.
  • Confirmed date, not a promise. Is the date and time window confirmed in writing, rather than a same-day or "we'll fit you in" assurance with no fixed slot?

3. Materials and Equipment

  • Who supplies what. Does the vendor bring their own cleaning materials and equipment, or does the client provide some or all of it?
  • Chemicals used. If surfaces are sensitive (electronics, specific flooring, upholstery), has the vendor confirmed what products they use on them?
  • Consumables. For a recurring contract, who restocks washroom or pantry consumables between visits, and is that billed separately?

4. Exclusions

A good quote is as clear about what is not included as what is. This is where cheaper quotes often hide their savings.

  • Named exclusions. Get an explicit list of what is not covered (repairs, pest control, waste disposal beyond bins, specialist equipment, exterior/high-access work).
  • Pest control is separate. Cleaning and pest control solve different problems and should appear as separate line items if both are needed, not bundled into one unclear price.
  • Damage and pre-existing condition. Does the quote note pre-existing damage or condition issues (stained carpet, cracked tile) before work starts, so there is no dispute afterward?

5. Schedule and Frequency

  • One-off or recurring. Is this a single deep clean or a repeating schedule (weekly, monthly, quarterly)?
  • What triggers a re-visit. If it is recurring, what happens if a scheduled visit is missed or delayed, on either side?
  • Seasonal flexibility. Does the schedule tighten during monsoon season (July to September) if the building sits near an unpaved lot or an active construction site, or is the interval fixed year-round regardless of how much dust and mud gets tracked in?
  • Change and cancellation. What is the notice period to change the scope, pause the schedule, or cancel the arrangement entirely?

6. Quote Terms and Payment

  • Everything in writing. Is the full scope, price and terms written down, not just quoted verbally over a call?
  • Payment structure. Deposit, on completion, or on invoice terms, and what percentage is due upfront?
  • What happens if something is missed. Is there a stated process for flagging a missed task and getting it fixed, or is the visit simply final once the crew leaves?
  • Price validity. How long is the quoted price valid for, if you need time to compare vendors before deciding?

7. Proof and Verification Questions to Ask

You do not need a vendor to hold a specific certification for the work to be done well, but you are entitled to ask what they can actually show you.

  • Liability cover. If a vendor claims to carry insurance, ask to see proof rather than taking the claim at face value.
  • Crew verification. Ask how the vendor vets or identifies the staff who will be on your premises, whether they can share crew CNIC copies with your security desk in advance, and how that changes for after-hours access.
  • References or prior work. Ask for a recent example of similar work, with the understanding that a vendor may not be able to share client names without permission.
  • Written record of the job. For anything beyond a routine clean, ask whether you get a written record of what was done and when, which matters if a dispute comes up later.

8. Red Flags to Watch For

  • Verbal-only quote. No written scope, exclusions or price, just a number over the phone.
  • Vague scope, specific price. A precise-looking number attached to "we'll clean the office," with no room-by-room or task-by-task detail.
  • Unverifiable claims. Certifications, memberships or regulatory approvals named without a way to check them. Ask directly for the registration or membership number and verify it independently before relying on the claim.
  • Full payment upfront, before any written scope. A deposit is normal; the full amount before you have seen the scope in writing is not.
  • Same-day promises with no confirmed slot. "We can come today" without a specific time window usually means the schedule has not actually been checked.
  • No named contact for problems. If something is missed, is there a specific person or number to raise it with, or does the trail go cold after the crew leaves?

The One-Page Version

Print or copy this list and check each item against every quote you collect:

  1. Rooms, floors and surfaces named exactly, not described generally
  2. Exclusions written down, not left implied
  3. Who supplies materials and equipment
  4. Access window, utilities and building restrictions confirmed
  5. One-off or recurring, and what happens on a missed visit
  6. Full scope and price in writing, not verbal only
  7. Payment terms and how long the quote stays valid
  8. Proof offered for any insurance, certification or membership claim
  9. A named contact if something is missed
  10. No full payment demanded before the written scope exists
