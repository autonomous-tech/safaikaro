Understanding Khatmal — Why Bed Bugs Are Uniquely Difficult to Eliminate

Bed bugs (Cimex lectularius) are flat, reddish-brown insects roughly the size of an apple seed. Unlike most pests, they have no connection to food scraps or drainage — they feed exclusively on human blood, emerging from hiding in the 1–2 hours before dawn when occupants are in deepest sleep. A Karachi home can develop a severe infestation without any evidence of poor hygiene.

What makes khatmal particularly resilient is their biology. Adult females lay one to five eggs per day, cementing them into seams, cracks, and fabric folds where they are nearly invisible. Eggs incubate for 6–10 days and hatch into nymphs that begin feeding — and hiding — immediately. A single pregnant female introduced on a second-hand mattress or a guest's luggage can establish a full infestation within two months.

Bed bugs are also highly hitchhiker-capable: they travel between flats in apartment buildings through wall sockets and skirting board gaps, making a whole-room (and often whole-floor) treatment approach essential rather than treating one mattress in isolation.

Khatmal ka ilaj — bed bug treatment — must be systematic, room-wide, and multi-session. Treating the mattress surface alone while ignoring the bed frame, headboard, skirting boards, and adjacent upholstery leaves 80–90% of the infestation untouched.

Why DIY Sprays and Repellents Don't Work on Bed Bugs

Off-the-shelf surface sprays, repellent strips, and camphor tablets are broadly ineffective against established bed bug infestations for three specific biological reasons:

  • Egg impermeability. Bed bug eggs are coated in a hard, waxy layer. Contact insecticides — even professional-grade ones — cannot penetrate this coating. Eggs simply wait out the spray and hatch days later.
  • Cryptic harborage. Bed bugs hide in locations that household sprays never reach: deep inside mattress tufting, inside electrical sockets and switch plates, behind loose wallpaper, inside hollow bed frame tubes, and beneath carpet edges. A surface spray treats 5–10% of where the population actually lives.
  • Dispersal under pressure. Repellents and strong-smelling products cause bed bugs to scatter from a primary harborage site into adjacent rooms and wall cavities — spreading an infestation that was previously contained.

Professional treatment is effective because it combines a residual insecticide (which remains active on treated surfaces for weeks) with an IGR that disrupts the moulting cycle of nymphs, ensuring that eggs which do hatch never develop into reproductive adults.

Applied alongside the residual spray. IGR is odourless and non-repellent — bed bugs cross treated surfaces without detecting it. It disrupts the hormonal pathway required for nymphs to moult into reproductive adults, stopping population growth at the source.

Follow-Up Session (Day 10–14)

The follow-up targets newly hatched nymphs before they can mature. This is the session that most DIY attempts skip — and the main reason infestations rebound after a single spray. SafaiKaro includes follow-up planning in every bed bug treatment.

Preparation — What to Do Before Treatment

  • Strip all bedding and launder at 60°C (or use a dryer on high heat for 30 minutes)
  • Clear clutter from under and around the bed to expose harborage points
  • Do not move furniture to other rooms — this spreads the infestation
  • Vacate treated rooms (with pets) for 3–4 hours post-application
  • Do not vacuum or mop treated surfaces for at least 72 hours after treatment
  • Leave mattress encasements in place if already fitted
