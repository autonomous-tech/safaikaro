Rats vs Mice — What's in Your Home and Why It Matters

Karachi homes typically deal with three rodent types, each requiring slightly different treatment approaches:

🐰

Norway / Brown Rat

Burrowing rat, common in ground floors, kitchens, and behind appliances. Karachi's older neighbourhoods and ground-level DHA homes see high activity. Aggressive gnawer — can chew through plastic pipes and soft concrete.

🐱

Roof Rat (Black Rat)

Agile climber found in upper floors, ceiling cavities, and roof spaces. Common in multi-storey homes in Clifton and DHA. Enters through gaps around pipes, AC units, and rooftop openings.

🦬

House Mouse

Smaller, can squeeze through gaps as small as 6mm. Often enters kitchens and pantry areas. Reproduces rapidly and contaminates more food per capita than rats due to frequent urination while moving.

Knowing the species matters because roof rats require treatment at ceiling level, while Norway rats are controlled at ground and wall level. SafaiKaro's technicians identify the rodent type during the first visit and place traps and bait stations accordingly.

Chuhe ka khatma — rodent control — is not just about hygiene. Rats gnaw through electrical wiring and plastic pipes. Wiring damage from rat activity is a documented cause of electrical fires in Pakistani homes. A rodent infestation is a structural and fire-safety risk, not only a health one.

What Rats and Mice Actually Damage

Beyond contaminating food and surfaces, rodents cause physical damage that compounds over time:

  • Electrical wiring: Rats gnaw through insulation on wiring. Exposed cables near insulation and dry wood are a direct fire hazard. This is the most serious structural risk of a rodent infestation.
  • Water pipes: Plastic supply pipes (CPVC, PVC) are targeted. Gnaw damage causes slow leaks and eventual pipe failure inside walls — expensive and often invisible until significant water damage has occurred.
  • Structural wood: Door frames, skirting boards, and wooden beams are gnawed to wear down continuously growing incisors. In older construction, this weakens joints and joinery.
  • Food contamination: Rodents urinate constantly as they move. Food stored without sealed containers in a rat-active kitchen is contaminated even without direct rat contact.
  • Disease transmission: Leptospirosis (from urine), hantavirus, salmonella, and rat-bite fever are all associated with rodent infestations. Young children and elderly family members are at highest risk from indirect contact.

Why Rodenticide-Only Approaches Don't Resolve Infestations

Poison bait placed without trapping or entry-point sealing is the most common reason rodent problems persist despite repeated treatments. Three reasons:

  • Not all rodents take bait. Rats are neophobic — cautious of new objects in their environment. Some will avoid bait stations entirely. Trapping catches individuals that bait does not.
  • Dead rats inside walls. Rodents that consume poison often die inside wall cavities or ceiling spaces, causing odour problems for weeks. Snap trapping allows removal of carcasses and keeps your home odour-free.
  • New rats replace old ones. Without sealing entry points, new rodents from outside replace those killed by poison. The infestation appears to reduce and then returns — and residents assume the treatment failed. In fact, the structural access was never addressed.

Effective rodent control requires three elements together: trapping, bait stations, and entry-point sealing. Missing any one of the three produces incomplete results.
